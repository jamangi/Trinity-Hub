# BO-04 — One-Task SOP Cards

Status: proposed; source procedure needed. Initial user: green jacket.

## Purpose

Make a frequent task understandable without rewriting the entire SOP collection. Clear prerequisites and expected observations reduce guesswork and repeated interruptions.

## Function

A short personal reference derived from an existing applicable procedure: purpose, prerequisites, ordered steps, expected observations, stop/escalation conditions, and source revision. Link back to the authoritative procedure. An unresolved gap remains a question, not an AI-authored instruction.

## Gather

- One frequently used procedure, its revision and applicability, and its owner.
- Missing newcomer context, terminology, required unit state, and known exceptions.
- Source-defined expected results, authorized actions, and stop conditions. See [Q05 and Q08](../context/open-questions.md).

## Build sequence and SIFT prompt

1. Select one narrow task whose ambiguity costs you time.
2. Use SIFT to identify unclear wording and produce a draft card with a source reference for each step.
3. Resolve gaps with the procedure owner; check that shortened wording has not dropped conditions.
4. Keep the source revision visible. Add more cards only for repeated needs; later a searchable card collection can link into the wiki.

> Turn the opened procedure into one concise task card. Preserve its prerequisites, order, conditions, expected observations, and stop rules. Cite where each step came from. Mark ambiguities as questions for the procedure owner. Do not create missing steps or simplify away exceptions. Include a comparison of what was shortened and what remains unresolved.

Apply the [common SIFT instruction](README.md).

## Machiavellian Index

- **Pain Addressed: 4/5.** Weak instructions repeatedly slow new associates.
- **Rank Needed: green for personal study.** A replacement or changed team procedure belongs to its owner.
- **Obnoxiousness: 1/5 personal; potentially 3/5 if presented as a new SOP.** Frame it truthfully as notes that helped you understand the existing procedure; credit its authors and reviewers.

## Try, measure, and decide

Compare the card line by line with its source and walk through a representative case with an experienced person before using it as guidance. Check that exceptions and stop conditions survived. Track clarification needs and stale revisions. Keep only cards that reduce confusion; retire one when its source changes until reviewed. You maintain personal cards; the procedure owner controls official guidance.
